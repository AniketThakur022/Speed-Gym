The bottleneck is missing taxonomy, not fuzzy names

The obvious hypothesis — that the corpus and the graph simply spell things differently — was tested and is wrong. Relaxing the matcher from exact to fully fuzzy (case, punctuation, word order, stopwords) moves attachment by 3 percentage points, from 18.0% to 21.0%. What actually blocks it is that 11,693 rows (59.6%) carry no topic label at all, and the whole corpus only ever names 63 distinct topics against a 422-skill vocabulary — it is tagged at chapter level, not skill level. No matcher can fix that; it is content work.

Quality of what does attach is also thin: 43% land on stub skills (real names with no metadata), and 33% land on skills that own no existing problems — those questions would be the first content under their skill rather than joining a pool.

The 22 topics with nowhere to attach

3,805 questions name a topic the skill graph has never had. They are not one problem — they are three, and only one of them is worth solving now.

CAT quant syllabus

7 topics · ~1,855 questions · mostly playable

Number Systems 612, Geometry and Mensuration 422, Profit and Loss 281, Interest 192, Progressions 168, Ratio and Proportion 140, Averages 40. Real exam mathematics the Vedic-first graph never covered. This is the family worth building skills for — it is on-band, in-demand and playable.

GRE verbal

3 topics · 524 questions

Reading Comprehension 210, Text Completions 166, Sentence Equivalence 148. Not mathematics at all. These need a separate vocabulary and a different mastery model — folding them into a maths skill tree would be a category error.

University mathematics

9 topics · 1,188 questions · 0 playable

Vector Spaces, Eigenvalues, Orthogonality, Linear Transformations (Strang); Binomial Coefficients, Special Numbers (Knuth); Probability Distributions (Hogg); Theory of Equations (Hall & Knight). Above the app's band, and not one of them is playable today. Creating skills here buys nothing.
